b) We can see contour plot lines roughly concentric around two points: $\left(-.61,-.45,1.60\right)$ and $\left(1.15,-0.95,-0.349\right)$ which points to the fact that these are likely stationary points on the surface. By evaluating the value of the function at either point, or by looking at the 3D visual  of the surface and locating the two points, we can find the nature of each stationary point. Since the z-value of $\left(-.61,-.45,1.60\right)$  is greater, it must be the maximum point of the surface, and $\left(1.15,-0.95,-0.349\right)$  must be the minimum point of the surface. Since the contour lines loop around the points in an approximately elliptical shape, these points cannot be saddle points. Note that the points mentioned are just approximations for the true maximum and minimum points of the surface found by visually observing the contour plots. To identify the true values, we would have to derivate the function which seems outside the scope of this problem set.
