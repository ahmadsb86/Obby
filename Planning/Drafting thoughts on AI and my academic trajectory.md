I've been interested in HPC for a while now, but really what keeps me motivated is seeing a marginal increase in performance. That's the whole goal. Unlike mathematics, human understanding has essentially no positive externality. No one needs to really know what exactly goes on when a program is run from end-to-end. Today, it's incredibly difficult for even an expert to tell you where the bottlenecks of programs lie. We use causal profilers, figure out what really is the rate-limiter and then go ahead and try to figure out an optimization. This leads me to worry that there is no value that human optimization has over a machine optimizing another machine. The arguments that mathematicians are currently making about the value of human understanding in it of itself really aren't applicable to such an applied field. I fear that continuing on my path of specializing in this stuff would essentially be living in denial because this domain consists of work that is easily externally variable, reiterable, and scalable (the perfect playground for AI systems) and the value only comes from results.

Additionally, my work really boils down to making a certain task in a large pipeline scheme faster and likely just more profitable for a firm. That is really demotivating for a student like me. I don't want to limit myself to a future with such a narrow meaning. This is one of the reasons I want to work in a domain like quantum computing. The youth of the field, coupled with its abstractness is motivating. It is far easier to have a vision for a downstream application that is genuinely useful in advancing the human condition. It is foreseeable that my work could be used to make quantum simulations more feasible and lead to drug discovery later on that could genuinely help the world.
