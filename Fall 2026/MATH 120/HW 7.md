# Problem 1

For an underestimate, we can construct imaginary rectangular prisms under the surface. The base of these prisms will be a $1\text{x}1$ square in the $x$-$y$ plane. Their height will be the approximate height of the surface at the top right corner of that $1\text{x}1$ square (towards positive $y$, positive $x$). Since there is a peak near the middle of the $R$, some boxes will inevitably overestimate while others underestimate. As it turns out, the overestimating boxes on the left and bottom of the peak outweigh the underestimating boxes on the right and top of the peak. Thus, the sum of these boxes is an underestimate
