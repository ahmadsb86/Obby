**Alcohols**

- **α cleavage:** C–C bond next to the C–OH breaks, giving an alkyl radical (lost) plus a resonance-stabilized O-containing cation (detected).
- **Dehydration:** loses H₂O, giving an alkene radical cation at **M – 18**. This peak is often small.

**Amines**

- **α cleavage:** C–C bond next to N breaks, giving an alkyl radical plus a resonance-stabilized N-containing cation.
- **Nitrogen rule:** an odd M⁺ means an odd number of N atoms (1 or 3). An even M⁺ means 0 or 2 N atoms.

**Halides** (isotope patterns, not cleavage)

- **Cl:** M and M+2 peaks in a **3:1** ratio.
- **Br:** M and M+2 peaks in a **1:1** ratio.
- If a fragment has a matching +2 peak, it still contains the halogen.

**Ketones and aldehydes**

- **McLafferty rearrangement:** requires an H on the **γ carbon** (three carbons from C=O). That H moves to O, the α–β bond breaks, and a neutral **alkene** is lost. The charge stays on the O-containing fragment (an enol radical cation).
- **α cleavage:** the bond between the carbonyl C and the α carbon breaks, giving an alkyl radical plus a resonance-stabilized **acyl cation (R–C≡O⁺)**.
